## google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite-20260721

**Benchmark standing.** First-party (Google DeepMind, GA since 2026-07-21, 1M input / 64k output): SWE-Bench Pro 54.2% vs 38.3% for Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, Terminal-Bench 2.1 54.0% vs 31.0%, MLE-Bench 39.2% vs 22.0%, GDPVal-AA Elo 1140 vs 642, OSWorld-Verified 74.0% vs 54.3%, CharXiv (no tools) 74.5% vs 73.2%, GDM-MRCR v2 128k 72.2% vs 60.1% and 1M pointwise 21.3% vs 12.3% (https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/flash-lite/). Google's own comparison table also puts it level with GPT-5.4 mini on SWE-Bench Pro (54.4%) and ahead of Claude Haiku 4.5 (39.5%) (https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/flash-lite/); it launched alongside Gemini 3.6 Flash on 2026-07-21 (https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-6-flash-3-5-flash-lite-3-5-flash-cyber/), covered at the time as Google's fastest, cheapest 3.5-family model for high-volume work (https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/21/google-gemini-flash-ai-mythos-rival.html; https://www.reuters.com/business/google-updates-lightweight-gemini-models-flagship-still-delayed-2026-07-21/). Artificial Analysis as surfaced through OpenRouter (`as_of` 2026-10-24): intelligence 22.2, coding 49.3, agentic 14.3 (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/benchmarks?source=artificial-analysis); AA's own page scores it 22 on the Intelligence Index, **#44/173**, speed #5/151 at 413.8 output tokens/s, $0.30/$2.50 (https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-5-flash-lite). OpenRouter's first-party eval runs (2026-10-01): GPQA Diamond 84.1% and τ-bench-verified airline 76.9% (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/benchmarks?source=openrouter). **Conflicting AA numbers:** a press review quotes AA at intelligence **36, rank 13/152**, 489.9 tok/s and 11.73 s time-to-first-token (https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/gemini-3-5-flash-lite-review-price-benchmarks) — irreconcilable with AA's current 22/#44/173, so treat any single AA figure as version-dated and cite the date.

**Opinions.** Google positions it as the low-latency/high-throughput agentic worker and quotes customer claims that it is "a huge jump from Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite" (Ashler/Palo Alto Networks/Ramp blurbs on https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/flash-lite/). The most-cited independent review argues the cheap tier now beats the *previous-generation* mid tier (SWE-Bench Pro 54.2 vs Gemini 3 Flash's 49.6, OSWorld 74.0 vs 65.1) but that "Flash-Lite is not beating the current mid-tier model, it is beating a previous generation one", and that the model's 11.73 s TTFT "is a genuine contradiction" for anything interactive (https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/gemini-3-5-flash-lite-review-price-benchmarks). Press coverage at launch framed it purely as the efficiency end of a three-model drop, with Gemini 3.6 Flash taking the attention (https://9to5google.com/2026/07/21/gemini-3-6-flash-launch/). I found no Reddit/HN thread specifically about this model — community signal is effectively absent.

**Known for.** Google's current cheapest general-purpose multimodal (text/image/file/audio/video in) reasoning model: 1,048,576-token context, GA, built for massive parallel call volume rather than single-call quality (https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/flash-lite/).

**Caveats.**
- *Traffic/selection:* it is **absent from the daily token rankings entirely** for 2026-09-25..2026-10-24 — verified against `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/datasets/rankings-daily` (no `google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite*` row; each day carries only 51 models with a cut-off of 42–61B tokens) — because its *usage* share is high while its *token* share is tiny (0.033 vs 0.003 within the classification-tagging tag, i.e. many short calls), so it is invisible to anything keyed on token volume. It does rank in the classified-traffic boards: **#10 in the research domain (0.06%)**, #6 in classification tagging, #10 in Q&A (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/classifications/task?window=7d).
- *Pricing structure (anchor correction):* $0.30/M in, $2.50/M out is the **base (standard) tier** as the brief stated, but there is also a flex tier at **half** that ($0.15/$1.25), an EU tier at $0.33/$2.75, a priority tier at $0.54/$4.50, and a separate `:batch` variant at $0.15/$1.25 (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite-20260721/endpoints; https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models). Flex is documented as "lower price, lower processing priority" and is only used if requested or admitted via `:floor` (https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/service-tiers) — so the flex discount trades availability/latency, which matters given the already-long TTFT.
- *Latency/quality:* very high throughput but long TTFT and weak far-end long-context retrieval (21.3% at 1M on GDM-MRCR) (https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/flash-lite/; https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/gemini-3-5-flash-lite-review-price-benchmarks) — wrong choice for interactive streaming or single high-stakes answers.
- *Routing/limits:* max completion is 65,536 tokens and `reasoning_effort` is supported on every endpoint; all eight endpoints are first-party Google capacity (Vertex global/EU/US, priority, or Google AI Studio) with no third-party reseller, showing 99.8–100% uptime over the last day (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite-20260721/endpoints).
- *Migration/deprecation (the successor question, verified first-party):* Google's deprecation table lists `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` (2026-07-21) with **no shutdown date**, and `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` (2026-05-07) with an earliest shutdown of **2027-05-07** and **recommended replacement `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`** (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations). **Conflict with AA:** AA's page says "This model is deprecated… Google has launched a newer release, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite. We suggest considering it instead" (https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-1-flash-lite-preview) — Google has *not* deprecated the GA 3.1 Flash-Lite, only warned it could be retired from 2027-05-07 (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations). Separately, the 3.1 Flash-Lite **preview** ID was shut down 2026-05-25 (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.1-flash-lite-preview), and Google now *limits* (but does not deprecate) the 2.5 Flash tiers, pointing new projects at "3.5 Flash-Lite or 3.8 Flash" (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations) — which contradicts the existing policy line that a 2.5-flash-lite sunset was "scheduled 2026-07-22".
- *Position in the family:* 3.1 Flash-Lite is still the volume workhorse on OpenRouter (2.109T tokens over the 30 days to 2026-10-24, #10 in the writing domain, #6–#10 across translation/data-extraction/summarization/classification tags) while 3.5 Flash-Lite has token volume below the rankings cut-off; AA's own comparison still serves 3.1's row as "deprecated, consider 3.5" (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/datasets/rankings-daily; https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/classifications/task?window=7d; https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-1-flash-lite-preview).

## qwen/qwen3.8-flash-20260826

**Benchmark standing.** **AA coverage gap confirmed:** there is no `qwen/qwen3.8-flash` row in the Artificial Analysis feed and none for its predecessor `qwen/qwen3.7-flash` either (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/benchmarks?source=artificial-analysis); a third-party comparison table also lists the hosted model's AA Index as "N/A" (https://www.respan.ai/articles/qwen-3.8-models-comparison). AA *does* benchmark the open-weights sibling **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next** at intelligence **40, ranked #9 of 121 models in its class**, "notably slow" at 52 tok/s and "very verbose" (240M output tokens per evaluation), 256k context (https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/qwen3-8-flash-next) — that is a different artifact from the hosted model below, so do not transplant the number. Vendor first-party numbers (QwenCloud, for the hosted `qwen3.8-flash`): SWE-bench Pro 62.5, DeepSWE 1.1 58.7, SWE-bench Multilingual 81.0, CoWorkBench 73.9, JobBench 55.7, Toolathlon 73.5, AndroidWorld 84.5, MathVision 95.7, LVBench 76.6 (https://docs.qwencloud.com/developer-guides/getting-started/latest-model); the Flash-Next card's shared table (used for both artifacts by third parties) also gives GPQA Diamond 91.7 and HLE 35.9 (https://www.respan.ai/articles/qwen-3.8-models-comparison). OpenRouter's own eval runs (2026-10-01): GPQA Diamond **88.6%** (n=198) and τ-bench-verified airline **71.3%** (n=50) (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/benchmarks?source=openrouter). Independent (non-vendor) vision eval: Roboflow Vision Evals, updated 2026-10-13 — overall **59.7%, #35/63**, 8th cheapest per sample ($0.0004), 12th fastest (7.34 s/sample), but **Reasoning 35.1% at low effort = #51/63** and OCR 58.9% (#32/47) (https://playground.roboflow.com/models/qwen/qwen3-8-flash).

**Opinions.** The two artifacts are deliberately different products: "the hosted Qwen3.8-Flash is a separate, more provisioned artifact… Licence, context default, built-in tools, even the thinking-mode toggle syntax all diverge", with Qwen's own model card saying the hosted version is "based on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next with more production features" (https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/qwen-3-8-flash-next-open-weights-vs-hosted-api-split; https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next/). One analysis calls it "the latency-tuned tier of this generation… built for readers who are sending a lot of calls and care more about what each one costs and how quickly it comes back than about topping a leaderboard", while warning that "Flash" is a tier name and that Alibaba publishes no matched per-token speed figures for it (https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/qwen-3-8-flash). Another notes thinking is on by default at `xhigh`, "and that default is where cost per task starts to drift away from the price sheet" (https://www.respan.ai/articles/qwen-3.8-models-comparison). Community discussion I could find is almost entirely about the **open-weights sibling** (r/LocalLLaMA megathread, llama.cpp/GGUF guides) rather than the hosted API (https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next/discussions/10; https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1vz87bj/how_do_you_run_qwen_38_flash_next_on_llamacpp/) — I found **no** independent community evaluation of hosted `qwen3.8-flash` tool-call reliability or latency on OpenRouter, and will not guess one.

**Known for.** Alibaba's hosted 125B/6B-active MoE flash tier (the production build of the Qwen4-architecture preview Qwen3.8-Flash-Next): 1M context by default with built-in tools, text/image/video in, sold for high-volume coding, agentic and multimodal-document work (https://docs.qwencloud.com/developer-guides/getting-started/latest-model; https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/qwen3.8-flash-20260826/endpoints).

**Caveats.**
- *Traffic/selection:* strong on the boards — **2.385T tokens over 29 of the 30 days** 2026-09-25..2026-10-24 (it missed exactly one day), **#12 in the coding domain (0.10%)**, #18 in research, #10 in file I/O, #6 in code review, #10 in math (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/datasets/rankings-daily; https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/classifications/task?window=7d) — but it is a mid-tail coding entrant, not a leader, so it needs an explicit summary to be picked at all.
- *Price vs the 3.7 entry (anchor question):* this is a **step up, not a cheap-utility successor** — $0.15/$0.47 flat versus qwen3.7-flash's $0.03/$0.13, i.e. ~5x the sticker; in exchange it has **no length-based price overrides at all** (catalog pricing carries no `overrides`), so unlike 3.7-flash — which costs 3.3x above 32K and 6.7x above 256K (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models) — what you see is what you pay at any prompt length. Cache reads are $0.016/M and cache writes $0.20/M (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/qwen/qwen3.8-flash-20260826/endpoints).
- *Provider concentration:* **single provider.** Both live endpoints are Alibaba (`alibaba`, `alibaba/us-east-1`) at identical price, both 100% uptime over the last day, with no latency/throughput telemetry published (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/qwen/qwen3.8-flash-20260826/endpoints) — no fallback pool if Alibaba degrades.
- *Caps/quality:* max prompt 983,616 tokens, max completion 131,072 (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/qwen/qwen3.8-flash-20260826/endpoints); thinking is on by default at `reasoning_effort: xhigh`, with `preserve_thinking` on, so multi-turn tool loops must round-trip `reasoning_content` or accuracy drops (https://docs.qwencloud.com/developer-guides/getting-started/latest-model). Speed is the weak side of the family: AA measures the sibling at 52 tok/s and calls it "notably slow" plus "very verbose" (https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/qwen3-8-flash-next), and a provider tracker reports a p95 time-to-first-token of 12.1 s on Qwen's own endpoint (https://llm-stats.com/models/qwen3.8-flash) — no OpenRouter-side measurement exists to confirm or refute that.
- *Licensing:* on OpenRouter it is a hosted proprietary call; the downloadable sibling ships under the bespoke **Qwen Community License 1.0**, which requires a separate licence from Qwen before running the weights commercially as a hosted inference service or inside an AI Work Assistant product (https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/qwen-3-8-flash-next-open-weights-vs-hosted-api-split; https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/qwen3-8-flash-next).
- *Evidence gaps:* no AA row (above); the architecture paper covers only the Flash-Next pre-training benchmarks, not the hosted product (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30320); no third-party tool-call error rate was found, so the 3.7-flash-style "8.88% tool-call errors" figure must not be inherited by association.

Expect the paid baseline to settle far below the headline number.
- *Traffic/selection:* in the 7-day classified-traffic window ending 2026-10-24 it is **#4 coding (0.52%), #4 ops, #6 orchestration, #12 research**, plus **#3–#7 within the individual tags** (devops #3, shell execution and repo scan #4, file I/O and security audit and frontend #5, workflow execution #6, code review #7) (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/classifications/task?window=7d) — a top-tier presence on the selection boards, which is why it must be summarized even though its paid volume is small.
- *Free-route question:* the model was **never free-only**, and is now paid-only. During the window the free route was **single-provider** ("MiniMax is the only provider") and rate-limited under OpenRouter's standard free-model limits, which "may have different rate limits or availability compared to paid versions" (https://freetokens.custats.info/offers/openrouter-minimax-m3-1-flash-free.html; https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/model-variants/free). - *Pricing/limits:* base tier $0.15 in / $0.60 out / $0.015 cache read, 1,048,576 context, no length-based price overrides (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models; https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/minimax/minimax-m3.1-flash-20261019/endpoints); on MiniMax's own API the price is flat at every prompt length, unlike M3 which doubles past 512K (https://minimax-ai.chat/models/minimax-m3-1-flash-preview/).
- *Effort/thinking:* reasoning is **always on** with efforts low→max (default **max**), and `effort: "none"` returns HTTP 400 — a real cost lever to pin before volume (https://www.eesel.ai/blog/minimax-m3-1-flash-review; https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M3.1-Flash).
- *Licensing:* open weights but **not** open source — MiniMax's own community licence requires prominent attribution on commercial deployments and **prior written authorisation once annual revenue exceeds $20M** (https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/minimax-m3-1-flash-open-weights; https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M3.1-Flash).
- *Evidence gaps:* AA's feed row has **null coding and agentic indices** (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/benchmarks?source=artificial-analysis), so its coding standing on OpenRouter's own feed cannot be shown; AA still classifies it as proprietary post-weights-release (https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/minimax-m3-1-flash); only two weeks of history exist and vendor scores are self-reported (https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M3.1-Flash).

## Policy summaries

**google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite**
Google's GA cheap multimodal tier (1,048,576 ctx, 65,536 max out, $0.30/$2.50 base, $0.15/$1.25 flex and `:batch`, $0.03/M cache reads, $0.014/web_search call, Oct 2026): first-party it beats 3.1 Flash-Lite by 16–23 points on SWE-Bench Pro (54.2 vs 38.3), Terminal-Bench 2.1 (54.0 vs 31.0) and OSWorld (74.0 vs 54.3) (https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/flash-lite/), while AA gives it intelligence 22.2 / coding 49.3 / agentic 14.3, #44 of 173 (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/benchmarks?source=artificial-analysis; https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-5-flash-lite). Pick it for high-volume, latency-insensitive call volume — classification, extraction, translation, parallel agentic-search sub-calls — and treat it as the migration target Google names for `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` (shutdown possible from 2027-05-07) (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations); do **not** pick it for interactive streaming or single high-stakes answers, because reported time-to-first-token runs to ~11.7 s and 1M-context retrieval is only 21.3% (https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/gemini-3-5-flash-lite-review-price-benchmarks; https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/flash-lite/). It sits at #10 in the research domain and #6 in classification tagging but appears in **no** daily token ranking for 2026-09-25..2026-10-24 (high call share, tiny token share), so volume-based signals will under-report it (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/classifications/task?window=7d; https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/datasets/rankings-daily).
