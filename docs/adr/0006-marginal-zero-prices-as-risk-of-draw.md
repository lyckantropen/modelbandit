# Marginal zero prices as risk of Draw

A Plan route ticket within its Allowance costs $0 in cash, and a $0 reaching
the score is division by collapse — the same defect ADR-0002 fixed for Free
routes. The companion decision: a marginal-zero ticket is never priced at zero;
it prices as risk of its Draw — Free risk times the ticket's list-priced Draw
for a Plan route, and, where there is no Draw (Free routes), Free risk times
the pool's cheapest paid ticket as before. The score therefore prefers the plan
while its Allowance holds (the fee is sunk; the Draw is the real cost) without
any route winning by division alone.

## Considered Options

- **List-priced within cap** (a plan ticket scores at its full list price) —
  rejected: it prices the sunk fee twice and erases the plan's advantage, so
  two servings of one model score identically and economics cannot tilt the
  draw — the entire point of the plan. The Draw stays the ledger's measure
  (opportunity cost), not the score's price.
- **Pool floor for all marginal-zero tickets** (every plan route prices like a
  Free route) — rejected: it flattens draw efficiency — burning cheap Allowance
  on a flash model scores the same as burning dear Allowance on a premium one —
  so the deal would draw the plan's scarcest budget first.

## Consequences

- One pricing rule covers Free and Plan routes; the Nominal floor remains its
  Free-route case, and the Free tests are unchanged.
- A zero-Draw plan serving (unlimited, free-in-plan) falls back to the
  pool-cheapest basis — the Free-route case — so no code path can price $0.
- The plan's advantage appears in the score exactly where it exists in the
  wallet: within Allowance, and gone at the cap.
